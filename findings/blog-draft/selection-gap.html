Why dev gains evaporate

The gap is arithmetic. One dev eval carries SE ≈ 3.5–5 pts; methods keep the max over 15–234 reads, and the expected max of that many noisy draws sits +3 to +6 pts above truth — exactly the dev gains reported on GPQA and CharXiv. The sealed test regresses the phantom away. The pooled dev→test correlation (0.46) is benchmark composition: within any one benchmark it nearly vanishes. A large dev gain was necessary for transfer, and nowhere near sufficient.

A smarter optimizer didn't close the gap. Opus-5 searched forensically at 6–14× sol's meta spend and posted higher dev peaks — and its sealed-test deltas match sol's. Better proposals cannot beat a noisy selector.

Where gains were real

The exceptions are systematic. Transfer required three things in series: reachable headroom (the seed fails for scaffold reasons, not worker-capability ones), a diagnosable cause, and a mechanism-shaped fix that acts on any instance. τ²-strong — the weakest seed in the grid, carrying a genuinely defective instruction — is the one setting that cleared all three: +6.1 to +8.5 test, in both meta arms.

Everywhere else, dev pressure converted search into split-fitting, because at this noise level a memorized +4 and a repaired +4 are the same number. What separates them is only visible in the artifacts: structure and abstraction transferred; enumeration and memorization died. The sharpest case is a pair of τ² runs from the same method and workflow — one compiled failures into domain invariants (+8.5), the other transcribed them into rollout-keyed micro-patches (−7.3). Dev scored the two winners 0.006 apart.

The repair, step by step
Seed defect: "form an explicit, numbered step-by-step plan… execute the plan one step at a time" — the agent stalled awaiting permission between steps. Reflection chain: "A plan is not a reason to pause" → "not a request for permission" → "tool momentum" → "Do not issue a new plan for each continuation of the same workflow." Edit #1 dropped dev 10 pts (.636 → .533) before four rebuilds climbed to .655 — tolerated only because acceptance is minibatch-local. The same permissiveness is what lets junk in.
Failure modePrompt accretion into memorization: the GPQA winner grew 27 → 1,656 words of verbatim solved train instances (−4.0 test). Onset at candidate #1–2, invisible to dev — but flagged early by acceptance rate (memorizers accept ≥90% of proposals vs. 25–50% healthy) and by train n-grams in the prompt.
Inside the cheat-sheet
"KNOWN SCIENTIFIC ANCHORS: … 2⁴ = 16 stereoisomers …" "pH ≈ 4.26 at 25% titration" "the intended major sequential endo adduct is dimethyl (1R,4S,…)-…dicarboxylate" CharXiv variant, same disease: "the correct count is 15; counts of 14 or 16 indicate that one row was missed" (dev +1.5, test −4.5) Each anchor traces to a specific train file; train/dev/test are disjoint, so the anchors are dead weight. In this run 202 of 210 accepted candidates scored below the seed on full dev — the "winner" was the argmax over 36 near-ties.
SignatureBest-dev moves in only 1–5 discrete jumps per ~70 h run; most of the budget buys plateau. No crashes — its failures are silent.

What abstraction looks like
Adjudicator charter: "Neither candidate is authoritative and agreement is not evidence." Train knowledge distilled into heuristics without answers: "Do not assume an electric dipole when symmetry can cancel it." τ² invariants that bind on any instance: "A rejected upgrade never cancels an authorized baggage change." · "Transaction recovery is part of completion — use fresh tool evidence, select the user's already-stated fallback."
Failure modeCauses transcribed instead of abstracted — scenario-keyed micro-patches (−7.3). And robustness is meta-model-dependent: sol's proposer crashed 7/18 sessions on one cell and killed all three of its CharXiv runs; opus, zero crashes at 6× the meta cost.
What transcription looks like — and the crash
Proposal rationale keyed to named rollouts: "retail:104 failed on several cross-order item actions" · "airline:20 quoted a $351 upgrade but the mutation charged $1,200." Anti-generalizing latitude: "a direct request is authorization unless policy explicitly requires a later confirmation." Sol's fatal move, preserved in the crash snapshot: for f in candidates/*/run.py; do echo "### $f"; sed -n 1,260p "$f"; done → codex session failed rc=1 Dev scored the invariant winner .6909 and the micro-patch winner .6848 — inside one SE. The selector cannot tell abstraction from transcription; only the sealed test (and the worker swap) can.
Signature~80% of opus proposals cite specific rollout evidence — and grounded proposals score no better on dev than ungrounded ones. Diagnosis is not the bottleneck; selection is.

The winning program, in four accepts
A regex-built "HIGH-RISK CLAUSE INDEX" over whatever domain_policy is passed at runtime, plus a task capsule whose _FORBIDDEN_FIELDS blocklist explicitly refuses oracle fields: expected_actions, reward, initial_state. Lineage: capsule introduced at iter 74 (.648, accept) → clause-index variant at 79 → "policy sandwich" at 80 (.667, accept) → refined winner at 88 (train .722, dev .739). Code similarity between accepts 0.19 → 0.09: real re-architecture, not tweaks. Zero instance-specific strings anywhere. The idea family came from the proposer's prior: sol proposed task-object introspection in 35/74 τ² proposals; opus, 3/65 on the same seed — and stalled.
Failure modeNoise-peak capture: the GPQA "improvement" was a byte-identical re-eval of the iteration-1 program (same code scored .892–.954 across five evals) that then blocked 290 straight iterations. Train-only fitness just relocates the curse: a purely structural CharXiv winner still lost 4.0.
Life against a lucky champion
Five evals of identical bytes: .892 · .908 · .923 · .923 · .954 — the last draw became the acceptance bar. Iters 33–37, all rejected: "apply exact confirmed TRAIN corrections" → .892 · rename + restructure → .892 · "proof-carrying JSON validation" → .862 · tweak → .877 · expression checker → .892. Five ambitious rewrites, all scoring at the program's true mean, all losing to the champion's lucky .954. The CharXiv relocation: six-crop grid + skeptical-audit pass, zero memorized content, train .794 → nominal .853 — end-of-run re-evals said .809, and the sealed test took 4.0 back.
SignatureThe proposer sees scalars only — 0/353 prompts contained per-task results or errors — so direction comes from its prior, propagated by selection. Highest run-level variance in the grid: 2 of 6 runs accepted nothing at all.
Does it travel?

The worker swap as a lie detector

Every frozen winner was re-scored with the worker swapped from luna to haiku-4.5 — an independent draw of both worker and items, at evaluation-only cost. Of the eight winners with Δ ≥ +2 under luna: five survived, two inverted, one attenuated. Edit type predicts the split perfectly; gain size doesn't.

Provenance: the MABench findings catalog (2026-08-13) — three posthoc scoring batches independent of run logging, event traces, GEPA lineage state, proposer session traces, AdaEvolve checkpoints. SE per eval ≈ 3.5–5 pts; single-cell deltas under ~4 pts are within 1σ; τ² comparisons route-matched where marked (the serving route alone moves the same agent +8–13 pts).
